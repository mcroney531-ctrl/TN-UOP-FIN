Beyond the Rollout

Long-Term AI Extensions for WN7 performance support

Two future concepts, not proposals. Neither is part of the WN7 rollout, and the immediate recovery problem doesn't need AI to solve it. They'd only be worth exploring once the recovery path is stable, the approved guidance is settled, and the pilot has shown what Guides actually struggle with. The first helps Guides practice before the call; the second helps them find approved guidance during it.

Concept 1 · Practice before the call

AI-Powered Scenario Simulator

The opportunity

Make the Call gives every Guide the same decision to practice. Real calls vary more: after a practice huddle, Imani noted that an eligible call, an ineligible one and a failed credit "sounded nothing alike after the first minute." A conversational simulator could eventually offer more varied customers for onboarding and refreshers.

What it would look like

A Guide talks with a simulated customer, chooses what to ask and say, and the customer responds the way that choice would lead. Feedback afterward maps the conversation to the six observable indicators and points back to approved guidance.

What it would take

Approved scenarios and model answers from Quality and Specialist Care; controlled model behavior so the customer stays in scope and never states policy; defined feedback criteria; testing for inaccurate or overconfident feedback; de-identified material only; and a limited pilot with Quality and Care stakeholders.

How we'd know it works

Review the quality of Guides' decisions and explanations in practice, then look for matching patterns on reviewed live calls using the same indicators. A practice result is a signal, not proof of workplace competence, and it wouldn't be used to grade Guides.

Concept 2 · Find approved guidance during the call

RAG-Powered Beacon Knowledge Assistant

The opportunity

The right article existed, but it was hard to reach mid-call. In Quality's Sep 22 search test, "Internet went out again" and "Video call froze" opened the older generic article first. Guides also had questions search couldn't settle: "I do not want to say it is active if the system is pending."

What it would look like

A Guide types a plain-language question and gets a short answer drawn only from approved Beacon content, with every statement cited and linked to its source. When approved guidance doesn't cover the question, it says so and points to the Lead or escalation route instead of guessing.

What it would take

Access limited to approved, current Beacon content; retrieval and citation design; freshness controls so superseded versions drop out; handling for conflicts and gaps; permissions that match who can see what; evaluation against a set of known questions with approved answers; and clear knowledge-governance ownership, with policy owners approving.

How we'd know it works

Check answer accuracy and source traceability against the known-question set; measure whether Guides find the right guidance more reliably than with search; and use unanswered questions as signals of content gaps. Any effect on call outcomes would need its own evaluation.

Boundaries for both concepts

  • Approved Beacon content and human governance stay authoritative. Neither tool sets policy.
  • The assistant answers questions only. It never determines eligibility, authorizes exceptions, issues credits, places replacement orders or runs transactions. Cam's caution on Sep 19 applies: no automated offer "before Product, Care, and Legal sign off."
  • The simulator is a practice environment, not a validated assessment.
  • Both would need feasibility work, stakeholder discovery, validation and evaluation before any deployment. No budget, timeline, vendor or approval is implied.
