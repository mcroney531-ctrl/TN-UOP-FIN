ANeeds assessment and problem framing

  1. A1Separate the need from the requested solution

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me (position); GPT (framing) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Treat Dana's video request as one possible solution and diagnose the performance problem first.
    Rationale
    Guides were asking for in-call help ("what I click and what I say"), several causes weren't about training, and completion wouldn't show whether calls changed. Dana also asked for "your best take on what Learning can put in supervisors' hands quickly."
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Build the five-minute video as requested. I didn't rule it out as a supporting resource.
    Consequence
    A needs assessment came before any format, and the video narrowed to an optional, conditional Orbit tool walkthrough.
    Working log: D35

  2. A2Analyze the workbooks before setting priorities

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Claude (flagged the gap); GPT (agreed) · Decided by: Me (approved)

    Decision
    Run a data pass on the two Orbit workbooks before locking design targets.
    Rationale
    The workbooks were the biggest unexamined source. They showed that 70 of 159 eligible calls with a recorded serial check still ended in generic troubleshooting.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Design from the qualitative notes alone. GPT later corrected an over-reading: the 44% figure supports investigating the check-to-action gap; it doesn't mean 44% of Guides need a video.
    Consequence
    The 70-of-159 figure anchors the Lead meeting (Case B) and the email, always framed as a recorded check, not proof of understanding.
    Working log: D64, D72

  3. A3Fix design targets before choosing formats

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me; GPT (review rounds) · Decided by: Me (locked)

    Decision
    Lock five design targets (end-to-end Guide performance, Lead calibration, in-call support, operational readiness, a small capacity-conscious pilot) before deciding any resource format.
    Rationale
    Picking formats first would have rebuilt the video request in other shapes. Each resource needed a defined job.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    GPT's proposed organizer structure was parked rather than adopted, and the working rule became no format until its job is established.
    Consequence
    Every later resource maps to one of the five targets.
    Working log: D65, D67, D68

BLead alignment and coaching

  1. B4Lead-anchored, but not a gate on Guides

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me; Claude (evidence); GPT (reframe) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Anchor the design in Leads while Guide support develops in parallel.
    Rationale
    Guides can't carry out a process their Leads haven't been aligned on, and Leads can't give consistent feedback without knowing what to look for. The release had already relied on Leads without equipping them.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    GPT's three-layer model (foundation, reinforcement, execution) was noted but not adopted. "First" was deliberately not treated as a gate.
    Consequence
    Lead alignment leads the rollout, and Guides apply approved guidance on live calls right away.
    Working log: D45

  2. B5Calibrate through cases, not a Lead knowledge test

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me (the question); GPT (assessment idea); Claude (evidence review) · Decided by: Me (working answer)

    Decision
    Treat Lead readiness as a gap in what reached Leads, and calibrate through realistic cases rather than testing WN7 knowledge.
    Rationale
    The packet showed strong knowledge in some Leads and uncertainty in others, with no baseline. Leads asked for cues and clips, not WN7 training.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    A three-dimension Lead readiness model with a WN7 knowledge assessment (GPT), not adopted because it would solve an unestablished problem. The gap between what Leads asked for and what they may need stays an open uncertainty.
    Consequence
    The meeting opens with a 3-minute verified-current-state refresh, and understanding shows up in the case discussion.
    Working log: D66

  3. B6Meeting length: 25, then 20, then 25 minutes

    Proposed · needs approval

    Raised by: GPT (25 minutes; agenda splits) · Decided by: Me (20, then back to 25; the final split)

    Decision
    A 25-minute working meeting, agenda 3 · 9 · 10 · 3, with the middle 19 minutes run as one exercise.
    Rationale
    Twenty minutes left too little room for the cases and alignment. The extra time goes to discussion, not presentation.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Twenty minutes, which better fit the evidence on Lead time ("one Lead could spare ten minutes"; Priya's "smallest manager behavior"). The trade-off is Lead time against one session replacing fragmented coaching later.
    Consequence
    The locked Lead Meeting Plan. Scheduling is subject to Workforce and Care leadership.
    Working log: D69, D79

  4. B7Two cases, and no second Plus case

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me (two cases); GPT (move the ineligible case); Claude (candidates, composite label) · Decided by: Me (locked)

    Decision
    Case A: a documented strong recovery where the Plus credit hadn't applied. Case B: a labeled composite in which the serial was checked but troubleshooting continued, ending with what the Lead would check first.
    Rationale
    One case shows the standard; the other practices diagnosing before coaching, which the 70-of-159 pattern called for.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    A second Plus case, rejected because Plus was already covered and it would have cost the diagnostic practice. The ineligible case moved to Guide practice.
    Consequence
    Case B is presented as a call record built from real data fields and labeled a composite, not as a real call.
    Working log: D73, D80

  5. B8Example bank becomes Coaching Cues

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me (excerpts idea); GPT (bank structure); Claude (no transcripts exist) · Decided by: Me (cheat sheet, renamed Coaching Cues)

    Decision
    Put coaching examples on a one-page Coaching Cues sheet rather than a separate excerpt bank.
    Rationale
    Leads asked for "a clip and a cue." The packet has descriptions of calls, not transcripts.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    A 3–5 excerpt bank of real calls. Only verified recordings would be labeled real excerpts; otherwise lines are illustrative.
    Consequence
    Coaching Cues uses sample Lead lines written as illustrations, not scripts.
    Working log: D74, D76

  6. B9INVESTIGATE before coach or escalate

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Claude (in drafting) · Decided by: Me (kept)

    Decision
    Add INVESTIGATE as a Lead response type when a Guide keeps troubleshooting after eligibility was verified.
    Rationale
    Coaching before the cause is found is the mistake the Lead materials are meant to prevent.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Only coach, escalate or flag.
    Consequence
    The Quick Reference response key, and the diagnose-first move in Coaching Cues.
    Working log: D105, D106

  7. B10Don't infer Orbit confusion from call timing

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Claude (original signal, in drafting) · Decided by: Me (corrected)

    Decision
    Replace the signal "a long pause, hold or transfer" with the Guide expressing uncertainty about which Orbit result counts.
    Rationale
    Call timing has many causes and shouldn't be read as Orbit confusion.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Keep the inferred behavioral signal.
    Consequence
    Coaching Cues wording.
    Working log: D108

CGuide Call Path and recovery requirements

  1. C11One authority, one job per resource

    Designer decision

    Raised by: GPT (design rules) · Decided by: Me (adopted)

    Decision
    Beacon owns procedures and policy; every other resource points there and has a single job.
    Rationale
    Uncoordinated chat notes, workarounds and drafts had already caused confusion (Sep 8). A second source of procedure would repeat that.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    A fuller Guide reference that restates BH-4419.
    Consequence
    The Call Path is deliberately short and non-procedural, and links to the Orbit panel directly rather than through Beacon.
    Working log: D76

  2. C12Three branches, including No clear result

    Designer decision

    Raised by: GPT (pending/unclear state); Claude (show all branches) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Show Eligible, Not eligible and No clear result, not just the recovery path.
    Rationale
    A recovery-only path teaches "WN7 = replacement", and about half of WN7-product calls in the data weren't eligible. Missing serials or an unavailable tool need a route, not a forced choice.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    A single recovery path, with ineligible handling left to Beacon.
    Consequence
    Branching in the Call Path, the practice and the huddle slide.
    Working log: D76, D84

  3. C13Don't fill policy gaps

    Required by case materials

    Raised by: Claude (source audit) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Treat anything not showing Applied as not complete; leave the eligibility-review follow-up expectation and the Lead-unavailable fallback to their owners.
    Rationale
    PO-117 covers a failed transaction, not a pending one, and doesn't define the follow-up expectation. A first draft wrongly implied it did.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Write the missing guidance ourselves.
    Consequence
    Before-release dependencies are shown inline, and the Ownership Map lists their owners. Owner confirmation is pending.
    Working log: D110; CALL_PATH_AUDIT

  4. C14"Check that the customer understands"

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me (wording); Claude (audit gap 3) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Use "Check that the customer understands the next step."
    Rationale
    More natural on a live call.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    PO-117's more specific "invite the customer to repeat back." The difference is recorded in the audit rather than hidden; the two don't contradict.
    Consequence
    Call Path wording, with a known, documented gap.
    Working log: D110, D111

DPractice and scenario design

  1. D15Individual practice replaces the micro-huddle

    Proposed · needs approval

    Raised by: Me + GPT; Claude (critique) · Decided by: Me (scope correction)

    Decision
    Replace the proposed 3–5 minute Guide micro-huddle with an individual digital scenario, keeping the team huddle and slide.
    Rationale
    It frees practice from another live session, works in scheduled time and tests application.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Claude first read the change as replacing the whole huddle and suggested dropping the slide; I kept both.
    Consequence
    Huddle and slide, then individual practice, then live Call Path use, then coaching. Sana delivery and practice time still need confirmation.
    Working log: D84, D87

  2. D16Not eligible as the primary scenario

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Claude (forces a decision); Rina, Sep 18 (evidence) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Build the practice around a WN7-pattern call whose serial returns Not eligible.
    Rationale
    Recognizing the pattern isn't eligibility; "the easy example always has a happy ending." It also needs no unapproved Plus wording.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    An eligible recovery, or a pending-Plus case, which is blocked on policy.
    Consequence
    Make the Call.
    Working log: D84, D111

  3. D17Fictionalize openly

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Claude (in drafting) · Decided by: Me (required explicit framing)

    Decision
    Add the WN7 symptoms to an ineligible case informed by a documented call, and say so.
    Rationale
    The documented call had a different symptom, and the sharper version better teaches "check, don't qualify."
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Present it as a reconstruction of the real call.
    Consequence
    The intro and call card state that it's fictionalized; the simulated Orbit result is labeled.
    Working log: D112, D113

  4. D18No scoring; completion isn't competence

    Designer decision

    Raised by: GPT · Decided by: Me (adopted)

    Decision
    Free-text decisions compared with model responses, with no grade. Opened or finished may be tracked as an operational measure only.
    Rationale
    A score would imply proficiency the exercise can't establish.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Scored quiz items.
    Consequence
    Delivery is reported as reach everywhere in the plan.
    Working log: D87

EHuddle design and delivery

  1. E19A lean, Lead-facilitated slide that keeps the Plus requirement

    Designer decision

    Raised by: GPT (keep it lean; later, "if applicable"); Claude (source check) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    One 16:9 slide Leads present: five steps, three branches, a recognition cue, and "For an eligible serial, both" at Complete.
    Rationale
    Finance, PO-117 and the locked Call Path all require both the replacement and the credit for an eligible serial.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    "Plus credit (if applicable)", rejected. A busier slide that becomes a second Call Path, avoided.
    Consequence
    The locked huddle slide (HTML + PDF).
    Working log: D84, D129, D130

FTime, coverage and capacity

  1. F20Route Specialist help through Leads

    Proposed · needs approval

    Raised by: Me (intent: a live safety net); Claude (routing); GPT (refinements) · Decided by: Me (two approvals under Mateo)

    Decision
    During initial rollout only, rotating Specialists may clarify questions in the Lead thread, as capacity allows.
    Rationale
    Specialist Care is the tightest capacity in the case, and a Guide–Specialist side chat would repeat the Sep 8 information sprawl.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Specialist shadowing, then a Guide-facing Specialist chat buddy: explored first, then replaced by routing through Leads.
    Consequence
    Mateo gives two separate approvals (A: the Lead meeting, B: the thread). If capacity allows only one, the Lead meeting comes first.
    Working log: D85, D94

GRollout, ownership and evaluation

  1. G21Define success before the plan; no numeric targets

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me; GPT (rename) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Write the success definition (Guide execution, customer recovery, operational capacity) before any rollout sequence, and test each phase against it.
    Rationale
    Each element should trace back to a success outcome. Numbers would be invented.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    An "Observable Execution Rubric", renamed to indicators because "rubric" implies scoring.
    Consequence
    Six observable indicators, and no thresholds anywhere in the proposal.
    Working log: D88, D89

  2. G22A baseline that can't deadlock the pilot

    Designer decision

    Raised by: Me + GPT · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Quality sets a defensible comparison method; where call evidence is missing, it states the baseline's limits instead of claiming indicator-level gains.
    Rationale
    Requiring a fully reconstructed baseline could block the pilot indefinitely.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    Making the full six-indicator baseline a launch prerequisite.
    Consequence
    Rollout plan pages 2–4.
    Working log: D93, D99

  3. G23Confirmed, proposed, to confirm, in three readiness tiers

    Designer decision

    Raised by: GPT (brief and review); Claude (flags) · Decided by: Me

    Decision
    Label every ownership line by evidence status, and separate launch blockers from evaluation-stage and only-if-used items.
    Rationale
    The organization shouldn't look more settled than the evidence supports.
    Alternative / tradeoff
    A RACI matrix, avoided. A thread Admin as a hard launch condition was made conditional on the thread opening.
    Consequence
    The Ownership Map and the Rollout Plan Overview.
    Working log: D122–D124, D126
