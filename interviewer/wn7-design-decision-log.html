ANeeds assessment and problem framing

  1. A1Separate the need from the requested solution

    Decision
    Diagnose the performance problem before choosing a format, treating Dana's video request as one possible solution.
    Rationale
    Guides were asking for help in the call itself ("what I click and what I say"), several causes weren't about training, and a completion count wouldn't show whether calls changed. A video could still be adapted or narrowed to a specific instructional need once that need was clear, so I didn't rule it out.
    Result
    The needs assessment came before any format, and the video narrowed to an optional, conditional Orbit tool walkthrough.

  2. A2Analyze the workbooks before setting priorities

    Decision
    Run a data pass on the two Orbit workbooks before locking design targets.
    Rationale
    After working through the threads, anecdotes and other qualitative material, the workbooks were the most significant evidence source still unexamined. Upon review, they showed that 70 of 159 eligible calls with a recorded serial check still ended in generic troubleshooting. I was careful not to over-read that: it points to a gap between checking and acting, not to 44% of Guides needing a video, and a recorded check isn't proof the Guide understood the result.
    Result
    The 70-of-159 figure anchors Case B in the Lead meeting and the evidence in Dana's email.

  3. A3Fix design targets before choosing formats

    Decision
    Lock five design targets (end-to-end Guide performance, Lead calibration, in-call support, operational readiness, and a small capacity-conscious pilot) before deciding any resource format.
    Rationale
    Choosing formats first would have rebuilt the video request in other shapes. Each resource needed a defined job before it got a form.
    Result
    Every resource maps to one of the five targets.

BLead alignment and coaching

  1. B4Anchor in Leads without delaying Guide support

    Decision
    Anchor the design in Leads while Guide support develops in parallel.
    Rationale
    Guides can't carry out a process their Leads haven't been aligned on, and Leads can't coach consistently without knowing what to look for. The release had already relied on Leads without equipping them. I didn't treat "Leads first" as a gate, though: holding Guide support until every Lead was aligned would have delayed help Guides needed.
    Result
    Lead alignment opens the rollout, and Guides apply approved guidance on live calls right away.

  2. B5Calibrate through cases, not a knowledge test

    Decision
    Treat Lead readiness as a gap in what had reached Leads, and calibrate through realistic cases rather than testing WN7 knowledge.
    Rationale
    Some Leads showed strong WN7 knowledge and others were unsure, with no baseline either way, and Leads asked for cues and clips rather than training. A knowledge assessment would have addressed a deficit no one had established. What Leads asked for may still not be all they need, so that stays an open question.
    Result
    The meeting opens with a three-minute refresh on the verified current state, and gaps surface in the case discussion.

  3. B6Meeting length: 25, then 20, then 25 minutes

    Proposed · needs approval
    Decision
    A 25-minute working meeting with a 3 · 9 · 10 · 3 agenda, the middle 19 minutes run as one continuous exercise.
    Rationale
    The first proposal was 25 minutes. I cut it to 20 because Leads have little spare time: one Lead could spare ten minutes, and Priya asked for "the smallest manager behavior that makes the learning stick." At 20 minutes the cases and alignment were too compressed. I ultimately judged the extra five minutes for Lead collaboration, discussion and alignment worth the investment, since one well-used session replaces fragmented coaching later.
    Result
    The Lead Meeting Plan. Scheduling is subject to Workforce and Care leadership.

  4. B7Two complementary cases

    Decision
    Case A: a documented strong recovery where the Plus credit hadn't applied. Case B: a labeled composite where the serial was checked but troubleshooting continued, ending with what the Lead would check first.
    Rationale
    One case shows the standard; the other practices diagnosing before coaching, which the 70-of-159 pattern called for. A second Plus case would have repeated ground the meeting already covers and cost the diagnostic practice, and the ineligible case fit Guide practice better.
    Result
    Case B is presented as a call record built from real data fields and labeled as a composite, not a real call.

  5. B8Coaching examples on one page

    Decision
    Put coaching examples on a one-page Coaching Cues sheet rather than a separate excerpt bank.
    Rationale
    Leads asked for "a clip and a cue." The case record describes calls but has no transcripts, so real excerpts are used only if Quality can supply verified recordings; otherwise the lines are illustrative.
    Result
    Coaching Cues uses sample Lead lines written as illustrations, not scripts.

  6. B9INVESTIGATE before coach or escalate

    Decision
    Add INVESTIGATE as a Lead response when a Guide keeps troubleshooting after eligibility was verified.
    Rationale
    Coaching before the cause is found is the mistake the Lead materials are meant to prevent.
    Result
    The Quick Reference response key, and the diagnose-first move in Coaching Cues.

  7. B10Don't infer Orbit confusion from call timing

    Decision
    Describe the coaching signal as a Guide expressing uncertainty about which Orbit result counts, not as a long pause, hold or transfer.
    Rationale
    Threads and operational notes pointed to several possible reasons for a hold, pause or transfer: asking a Lead about the offer, access or permissions, a delayed credit system. Timing alone isn't evidence that a Guide misunderstood Orbit, and coaching on that assumption could miss the real cause.
    Result
    The Coaching Cues signal is based on what the Guide says, not how long the call took.

CGuide Call Path and recovery requirements

  1. C11One authority, one job per resource

    Decision
    Beacon owns procedures and policy; every other resource points there and has a single job.
    Rationale
    Beacon was already familiar to Guides. Uncoordinated notes, workarounds and drafts had already caused confusion (Sep 8), and another procedural authority would add complexity and risk conflicting guidance.
    Result
    The Call Path is deliberately short and non-procedural, and links straight to the Orbit panel rather than routing through Beacon.

  2. C12Keep all three branches

    Decision
    Show Eligible, Not eligible and No clear result, not only the recovery path.
    Rationale
    A recovery-only path teaches "WN7 = replacement," and about half of WN7-product calls in the data weren't eligible. A missing serial or an unavailable tool needs a route, not a forced choice.
    Result
    The same three branches run through the Call Path, the practice and the huddle slide.

  3. C13Don't fill policy gaps

    Required by case materials
    Decision
    Treat a Plus credit that doesn't show Applied as not complete, and leave the eligibility-review follow-up expectation and the Lead-unavailable fallback to their owners.
    Rationale
    PO-117 covers a failed transaction, not a pending one, and doesn't define the follow-up expectation. An early draft implied it did. Writing plausible answers ourselves would have put unapproved policy in front of Guides.
    Result
    Before-release dependencies are shown inline, and the Ownership Map names who must confirm each one.

  4. C14"Check that the customer understands"

    Decision
    Use "Check that the customer understands the next step."
    Rationale
    It's more natural on a live call. PO-117's "invite the customer to repeat back" is more specific; the difference is documented in the source audit rather than hidden, and the two don't conflict.
    Result
    The Call Path's closing check.

DPractice and scenario design

  1. D15Individual practice replaces the micro-huddle

    Proposed · needs approval
    Decision
    Replace the proposed 3–5 minute Guide micro-huddle with an individual digital scenario, keeping the team huddle and slide.
    Rationale
    Individual practice fits scheduled work time, avoids another live session and tests application. I kept the team huddle as the place Leads introduce the change and open the conversation.
    Result
    Huddle and slide, then individual practice, then live Call Path use, then coaching. Sana delivery and practice time still need confirmation.

  2. D16Not eligible as the primary scenario

    Decision
    Build the practice around a WN7-pattern call whose serial returns Not eligible.
    Rationale
    Recognizing the pattern isn't eligibility, and "the easy example always has a happy ending." The scenario asks Guides to apply judgment and change behavior rather than memorize the recovery steps, and it needs no unapproved Plus wording. It's openly fictionalized: informed by a documented ineligible call, with the WN7 symptoms added, and it says it isn't a reconstruction of that call.
    Result
    Make the Call.

  3. D17No scoring; completion isn't competence

    Decision
    Free-text decisions compared with model responses, with no grade. Opened or finished may be tracked as an operational measure only.
    Rationale
    A score would imply proficiency the exercise can't establish.
    Result
    Delivery is reported as reach throughout the plan.

EHuddle design and delivery

  1. E18A lean, Lead-facilitated slide that keeps the Plus requirement

    Decision
    One 16:9 slide Leads present: the five steps, three branches, a recognition cue, and "For an eligible serial, both" at Complete.
    Rationale
    Finance, PO-117 and the Call Path all require both the replacement and the credit for an eligible serial, so softer wording ("if applicable") was rejected. Keeping the slide lean stops it from becoming a second Call Path.
    Result
    One shared picture of the sequence that Leads use to introduce the change in their huddle and point Guides to their practice.

FTime, coverage and capacity

  1. F19Route Specialist help through Leads

    Proposed · needs approval
    Decision
    During initial rollout only, rotating Specialists may clarify questions in the Lead thread, as capacity allows.
    Rationale
    Specialist Care is the tightest capacity in the case, and a Guide-facing Specialist channel would repeat the Sep 8 information sprawl. Shadowing and a Guide chat buddy were explored first; routing through Leads keeps one support path.
    Result
    Mateo gives two separate approvals (A: the Lead meeting, B: the thread). If capacity allows only one, the Lead meeting comes first.

GRollout, ownership and evaluation

  1. G20Define success first, without letting the baseline block the pilot

    Decision
    Define observable success (Guide execution, customer recovery, operational capacity) before sequencing the rollout, and have Quality set a defensible comparison method.
    Rationale
    Each phase should trace back to a success outcome, and numeric targets would have been invented. The available baseline is a call sample with eligibility classified after the fact, and some underlying call evidence may not exist. Requiring a full six-indicator baseline could block the pilot indefinitely, so where evidence is missing, Quality states the limits instead of claiming indicator-level gains.
    Result
    Six observable indicators, no thresholds, and an evaluation approach that's explicit about what it can't prove.

  2. G21Confirmed, proposed, to confirm, in three readiness tiers

    Decision
    Label every ownership line as confirmed, proposed or to confirm, and separate launch blockers from evaluation-stage and only-if-used items.
    Rationale
    The organization shouldn't look more settled than the evidence supports. Naming unresolved ownership also opens room for stakeholder collaboration, process refinement and agreement before launch; it isn't just an inventory of missing approvals.
    Result
    The Ownership Map and the Rollout Plan Overview.

HPackaging and communication

  1. H22Three high-level overviews before the detail

    Decision
    Organize the package in two top-level sections: 01 — Proposal (Start Here with three high-level overviews, Needs Assessment & Planning, Learning Resources) and 02 — UOP (the interviewer material). This structure was refined during final assembly from an earlier three-folder plan.
    Rationale
    Dana should be able to orient herself in minutes before the five-page plan.
    Result
    The package structure and the email's navigation.

  2. H23Persuade, then navigate

    Decision
    Open the email with my needs assessment and the evidence, then the rationale, then the folders.
    Rationale
    The email has to persuade Dana to open the materials, not just list them, so the performance problem and the reasoning come before the folder structure.
    Result
    Dana's pre-read email leads with the performance gap and supporting evidence, explains why the proposed approach goes beyond the original video request, and then guides her through the proposal package. The structure establishes the case for the intervention before introducing the resources.
