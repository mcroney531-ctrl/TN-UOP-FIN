Hi Dana,

Thank you for sending Renee's Orbit exports and the background. I've reviewed your request for a short video to help Guides navigate difficult customer conversations, along with the data, internal discussions, and feedback from Guides and Leads. Before recommending a solution, I wanted to better understand the underlying performance challenges and what might be contributing to them.

Overall, my needs assessment suggests that the challenge extends beyond helping Guides manage upset callers. The WN7 data points to a broader gap between identifying customers who qualify for recovery and successfully completing that process. Addressing that gap will likely require more than a single training intervention, which is why I'm recommending a Lead-first approach supported by targeted learning resources and operational alignment. Below is the evidence behind that, and a guide to the attached package.

**What the evidence shows**

In a systematic Orbit sample of 204 eligible General Care calls from September 21–30, 115 (56%) went through generic troubleshooting instead of recovery. Even among the 159 calls with a serial check recorded, 70 still ended in generic troubleshooting.

A recorded check can't tell us what the Guide saw or understood, so this isn't a proven root cause. But awareness alone doesn't appear to explain the gap. The gap may involve recognizing the pattern, interpreting the Orbit result, and translating that result into the right recovery action. That matters for upset callers too: a customer asked to repeat restarts they've already tried has good reason to be frustrated.

**Why I'm proposing more than a video**

A short video could explain the recovery path well. I don't think explanation alone will change what happens on live calls. At the September 18 shift feedback session, Micah described not remembering whether the offer included a subscription or a discount, then holding to ask a Lead: "the time on the call nobody sees when they say 'just use the script.'" Priya summed up the session as "a search problem, an eligibility problem, a conversation problem, and a completion problem."

Only part of that is a training problem. Guides also need consistent recovery guidance, alignment on Plus handling, a working Orbit workflow and access, a reliable route to their Lead when they're stuck, and reinforcement based on what happens on real calls. That's why the package is a small set of connected supports rather than one course. It's also built to answer Marisol's point from October 1: more than five minutes is acceptable if it helps Guides and protects service levels, but there has to be a reason to believe it will work.

**Why Leads go first**

Guides go to their Leads for answers mid-call, so any gap in what Leads know shows up directly on calls. With roughly 900 hourly Guides on rotating schedules and limited Specialist capacity, pulling everyone into a session at once would cost real phone time without telling us whether it works.

So the proposal starts with Leads. A 25-minute working meeting built on two cases (one documented call, one composite drawn from the call data) gives them one shared standard for a complete recovery and for when to coach or escalate. Leads then introduce the sequence in the huddles they already run, using a single slide. Guides complete a short individual practice in scheduled work time and use the Call Path on live calls. The goal isn't more training for everyone. It's consistency, targeted support, and a pilot of two or three Lead teams (proposed; Workforce confirms) that shows what actually changes before anything expands.
