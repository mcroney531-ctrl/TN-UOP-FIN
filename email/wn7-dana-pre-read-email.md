The WN7 call data reveals a gap between identifying eligible customers and completing recovery, while feedback across Guides, Leads and leadership offers insight into the challenges contributing to that gap. Addressing that gap will likely require more than a single training intervention, which is why I'm recommending a phased approach that begins with aligning Leads, supported by targeted learning resources and operational coordination. Below is the evidence behind that, and a guide to the attached proposal package.

**What the evidence shows**

In a systematic Orbit sample of 204 eligible General Care calls from September 21–30, 115 (56%) went through generic troubleshooting instead of recovery. Even among the 159 calls with a serial check recorded, 70 still ended in generic troubleshooting.

A recorded check can't tell us what the Guide saw or understood, so this isn't a proven root cause. But awareness alone doesn't appear to explain the gap. The gap may involve recognizing the pattern, interpreting the Orbit result, and translating that result into the right recovery action. That matters for upset callers too: a customer asked to repeat restarts they've already tried has good reason to be frustrated.

**Beyond training alone**

While a short video could communicate the recovery process, I don't believe explanation alone will be enough to change what happens during live calls. In a September shift feedback session, one Guide described going on hold to ask a Lead because they couldn't recall whether the offer included a subscription or a discount. Priya characterized the broader challenge as "a search problem, an eligibility problem, a conversation problem, and a completion problem."

Only part of that is a training problem. Guides also need consistent recovery guidance, alignment on Plus handling, a working Orbit workflow and access, a reliable route to their Lead when they're stuck, and reinforcement based on what happens on real calls. That's why the package is **a small set of connected supports rather than one course**. It also aligns with Marisol's October 1 guidance: additional learning time is worth considering if it improves Guide performance without compromising service levels, provided there's evidence to support the approach.

**Building alignment through Leads**

Guides go to their Leads for answers mid-call, so any gap in what Leads know shows up directly on calls. With roughly 900 hourly Guides on rotating schedules and limited Specialist capacity, pulling everyone into a session at once would cost real phone time without telling us whether it works.

The proposed rollout begins by establishing a shared understanding among Leads of the recovery process, coaching expectations, and escalation routes. A 25-minute working meeting built on two cases (one documented call, one composite drawn from the call data) is where that happens: Leads leave with one standard for a complete recovery and for when to coach or escalate. Leads then introduce the sequence in the huddles they already run, using a single slide. Guides complete a short individual practice during scheduled work time and use the proposed Guide Call Path as an in-the-moment reference during live calls. Rather than assigning broad, one-size-fits-all training across all 900 Guides, I recommend targeted support and a pilot with two or three Lead teams, subject to Workforce approval, to evaluate what changes before expanding. As the approach scales, the goal is to strengthen General Care's ability to resolve eligible WN7 calls independently, gradually reducing avoidable escalations to Mateo's Specialists.
