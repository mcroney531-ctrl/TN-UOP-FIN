Hi Dana,

Thank you for sending Renee's Orbit exports and the background. You asked for a short video to help Guides handle upset callers without losing control of the conversation. When I worked through the material, much of the long-call pattern traced back to one place: calls about the WN7 hub, where eligible customers often aren't reaching the recovery path. I want to share what I found and what I'd propose before you read the attached materials.

**What the evidence shows**

In a systematic Orbit sample of 204 eligible General Care calls from September 21–30, 76 ended in a complete recovery (37%) and 13 in an incomplete one (6%). The other 115 (56%) went through generic troubleshooting instead. Eligibility in that sample was classified afterward, so these are pre-rollout findings, not a performance score.

The more telling figure is this: 159 of those calls had a serial check recorded, and 70 of them still ended in generic troubleshooting. So in many calls the article was found and the check was run, but the call didn't move to recovery. A recorded check can't tell us what the Guide saw or understood. Still, it points to a working diagnosis: awareness isn't the main obstacle. Guides are struggling to recognize the pattern, read the Orbit result, and act on it consistently. Notes from the same weeks fit that picture: a Guide who didn't know which value on the Orbit screen counted as the result, and a call where it wasn't clear whether the Guide lacked access or didn't know the screen.

That also connects to the upset callers. When a Guide asks someone to restart a hub they've already restarted three times, the frustration is partly created by the path the call is on. As Asha put it, the customer told her, "If this is another reset, I am done."

**Why I'm proposing more than a video**

A short video could explain the recovery path well. I don't think explanation alone will change what happens on live calls. At the September 18 shift feedback session (volunteers' own accounts, not a rate), Imani said it directly: "People can tell you all five CLEAR steps in a quiz. The issue is the pivot from listening to acting." Micah described the cost of guessing: unsure whether the offer included a subscription or a discount, he went on hold to ask a Lead, "the time on the call nobody sees when they say 'just use the script.'" Priya summed up that session as "a search problem, an eligibility problem, a conversation problem, and a completion problem."

Only part of that is a training problem. Guides also need consistent recovery guidance, alignment on Plus handling, a working Orbit workflow and access, a reliable route to their Lead when they're stuck, and reinforcement that looks at what happens on real calls. That's why the package is a small set of connected supports rather than one course, and why it names the owners who need to confirm each dependency. Marisol said on October 1 that five minutes isn't a ceiling if more time helps Guides and service levels are protected, and that she needs a reason to believe it will work. I've tried to build toward that reason.

**Why Leads go first**

Guides go to their Leads for answers mid-call, so any gap in what Leads know shows up directly on calls. With roughly 900 hourly Guides on rotating schedules and limited Specialist capacity, pulling everyone into a session at once would cost real phone time without telling us whether it works.

So the proposal starts with Leads. A 25-minute working meeting built on two cases (one documented call, one composite drawn from the call data) gives them one shared standard for what a complete recovery looks like and when to coach or escalate. Leads then introduce the sequence in the huddles they already run, using a single slide. Guides complete a short individual practice in scheduled work time and use a one-page Call Path on live calls. The goal isn't more training for everyone. It's consistency, targeted support, and a pilot of two or three Lead teams (proposed; Workforce confirms) that shows us what actually changes before anything expands.

**What's included**

The package is organized in three folders.

*01 — Start Here.* Three short documents for orientation before you go deeper:
- **Resource Overview:** what each resource is, who it's for, and how they fit together.
- **Rollout Plan Overview:** the five proposed phases, the evidence we'd watch, and what decides each step forward.
- **Ownership Map Overview:** who leads and supports each part, what's confirmed versus proposed, and the decisions still open.

*02 — Detailed Planning.* The full **WN7 Rollout & Reinforcement Plan** (PDF): the analysis and rationale, rollout phases and their gates, the evaluation approach, operational dependencies, and what needs confirming before launch.

*03 — Learning Resources.* The proposed materials themselves. For Leads: the **Lead Meeting Plan**, **Lead Quick Reference** and **Coaching Cues**, which align Leads and support coaching in call reviews. For Guides: the **Guide Call Path** for in-the-moment support and **Make the Call**, an individual practice built around the harder Not eligible case. The **Huddle Sequence Slide** connects the two in the huddles Leads already run.

**Where to start**

I'd suggest starting with 01 — Start Here. The three overviews take a few minutes together and will tell you whether you want the full plan, the learning resources, or both.

A few things to keep in mind as you read. This is a proposal, not an approved rollout. Several pieces depend on confirmation from their owners before any Guide sees them, including the Plus guidance, the Orbit workflow and General Care access, delivery of the practice through Sana, and pilot teams and practice time. The rollout is staged, so pilot evidence decides whether and how it expands. And it's modular: Guides would see the Call Path, the practice and the huddle slide, while the meeting plan and coaching materials are for Leads.
